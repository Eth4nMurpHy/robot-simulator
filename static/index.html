HOW THE MODEL WORKS

A scheduling sandbox, with explicit assumptions.

Use Map editor to change edge lengths and My strategy to edit or import explicit robot commands. For command plans, the engine reports resource conflicts instead of delaying commands. Automatic examples reserve resources themselves. This simulator implements the minimum deliverable described in your EE6228 dual-loop proposal. It models four robots, one exclusive intersection, one or two shared charging bays, task deadlines, and event-based battery consumption.

01 · Load your tasks

Generate a reproducible list or import CSV/JSON. All time fields are in seconds and battery levels are percentages. R1/R2 serve A stations; R3/R4 serve B stations. Cross-zone deliveries are rejected.

02 · Choose a scheduling rule

FCFS: considers tasks by release time and assigns the compatible robot with the earliest predicted completion. Charges below 20%, or sooner for safety.

Deadline: considers earliest deadlines first, using priority as a tie-breaker. It reserves intersection and charging access while planning each task.

Joint greedy: evaluates the next eight tasks and both compatible robots together. Score = completion seconds + 3 × priority × lateness seconds + 0.5 × resource wait seconds + 2 × energy percentage points. This is a heuristic, not CP-SAT or a global optimum.

03 · Understand energy & safety

Ordinary edges use global defaults, or your edited map’s lengths, speed, and energy per meter. Crossing edges use the crossing duration and proportional energy. Loading and unloading each consume 0.5%. Queueing consumes energy; powered-down idle between jobs consumes none. Charging restores battery to 95% at a constant rate.

In command mode, you assign tasks and request charging explicitly. Commands start at exactly their requested times; busy robots, overlapping intersection/charger use, and low battery reject the plan. Uncommanded tasks remain unexecuted.

For automatic examples only: before assigning a task, the planner checks enough battery remains to finish it, reach a charger, and wait for a reserved bay. It additionally retains conservative queue headroom for three other robots each needing a full recharge; this can cause earlier charging. If needed, it charges first. Infeasible inputs are reported rather than silently allowing low battery.

04 · Read the output

Makespan is the final delivery time. Average wait is total intersection and charging queue time divided by task count. Energy is cumulative consumption in battery percentage points across all robots. Utilization is occupied resource time divided by makespan (and bay count for charging).

Reservations include a safety gap of twice the per-side buffer between crossings. Ordinary loop edges are not exclusive: this model checks resource conflicts, not continuous geometry, rear-end collisions, or physical traffic dynamics.

Replay is evidence, not a second simulation. All robot positions, battery curves, resource states, schedules, and exports come from the same action log. Uploaded tasks stay in your browser and this local server; they are not sent to an external service.

Not included: global CP-SAT optimization, live dynamic order injection, nonlinear charging, vehicle kinematics, or repeated-seed statistical experiments. Strategy improvements are workload-dependent.
